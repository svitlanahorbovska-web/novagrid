The Return of Small, Strange Games

Independent studios keep taking the biggest risks.

While franchises get louder, bigger and more expensive, some of the most interesting ideas in games right now are coming from teams of three or four people working out of a shared apartment. Small budgets force small teams to lead with a single sharp idea instead of ten safe ones - and increasingly, players are noticing.

These games rarely chase realism or scale. They chase feeling - a strange color palette, an uncomfortable mechanic, a story that doesn’t resolve neatly. That’s exactly what makes them memorable in a landscape saturated with polish.

It’s not nostalgia for a scrappier era. It’s a reminder that the most experimental work in any medium usually comes from the edges, not the center - and that games are no exception.
